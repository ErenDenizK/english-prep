## Session I — Use of English & Reading

**40 questions, every one worth 1.5 points. 60 points total.**

| | Items | Points | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cloze test | 10 | 15 | 1–10 |
| Closest meaning | 10 | 15 | 11–20 |
| Reading — two texts, 7 questions each | 14 | 21 | 21–34 |
| Paragraph completion | 6 | 9 | 35–40 |

Everything is four-option multiple choice.

### Cloze test — one passage, ten numbered blanks

A single continuous text (~450 words in the sample) with ten blanks. Each
blank has its own four options printed after the passage. The options are
*not* four forms of one verb — they are four different words or
structures, and each blank tests something different.

The grammar the sample actually tests, blank by blank:

| | Tests | In the app? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | discourse markers (`Similarly / Nevertheless / In spite of / Since`) | no |
| 2 | modals (`used to / must not / should / did not have to`) | **yes** |
| 3 | causative `make + object + bare infinitive` | partly |
| 4 | modals (`should not / do not have to / used to / had better`) | **yes** |
| 5 | vocabulary (`consumerism / gratitude / conflict / generosity`) | no |
| 6 | comparatives (`as advanced as / less advanced than / …`) | no |
| 7 | `so / such` (`so smoothly / such a smooth / so smooth`) | no |
| 8 | relative pronouns (`whom / whose / which / that`) | no |
| 9 | quantifiers (`a few / much / plenty of / a little`) | no |
| 10 | vocabulary (`appreciate / devastate / smuggle / emerge`) | no |

**This is the finding that matters most in the whole round.** Two of ten
blanks are modals; the app's three topics are Tenses, Modals and Passive
Voice, and *not one blank in the sample cloze tests a tense or the
passive*. The grammar this section actually rewards is discourse markers,
relative clauses, quantifiers, comparatives, `so/such` and causatives —
almost exactly the set the app does not cover. The learning-design arm
predicted this from published error analyses of Turkish learners; the
paper confirms it from the other side.

### Closest meaning — sentence restatement

One sentence, four paraphrases, choose the one that means the same. This
is not a vocabulary item: the distractors are grammatically fluent and
differ in *meaning*, usually by reversing a causal direction, changing a
modality, or swapping which of two things is being compared.

What the sample turns on: future perfect, third conditional, passive
reporting (`It is claimed by …`), modal perfects (`could not have
originated`), `unless`, correlative comparatives (`The more … the less`),
`as … as`, `enough to`, and concession (`in spite of`, `despite`).

The app's Modal Perfects and Passive Reporting lessons land here directly.
Conditionals and comparatives — neither covered — carry more of it.

### Reading — two texts, seven questions each

Both sample texts run to roughly 700 words and are **divided into
paragraphs numbered with Roman numerals**, because most questions cite a
paragraph explicitly. That numbering is part of the item, not typography.

The seven question types across the two texts:

| Type | Example stem |
| --- | --- |
| Detail | "According to paragraph I, … shows that ………" |
| Negative detail | "According to paragraph III, which one of the following is **NOT TRUE**?" |
| Inference | "According to paragraph VII, which of the following can be inferred?" · "We understand from paragraph II that ………" |
| Vocabulary in context | "In paragraph V, the word *steer* is closest in meaning to ………" |
| Reference | "In paragraph V, the word *its* refers to ………" |
| Paragraph main idea | "Paragraph VI mainly discusses ………" |
| Author's purpose | "The purpose of the author in writing this text is ………" |

Each text carries roughly one vocabulary-in-context item, one reference or
main-idea item, one or two inference items, and the rest detail.

### Paragraph completion — a sentence removed from a paragraph

A paragraph of roughly 120 words with one sentence removed, and four
candidate sentences. **The gap is not always in the middle**: of the six
sampled, one has it at the very start of the paragraph, the rest in the
middle or at the end. An item whose gap is first is a different task —
the learner has to infer the topic sentence from what follows.

Distractors are all on-topic and all grammatical. They fail on coherence:
wrong referent, a detail the paragraph never picks up, a contradiction of
the following sentence.

## Session II — Listening

**20 points**, in two parts.

- **While-listening**, 6 points (6 × 1). Six four-option multiple-choice
  questions answered *during* the lecture. Questions 41–46 in the sample.
- **Note-taking**, the remaining 14 points. Not multiple choice: a printed
  **note-taking sheet** with the lecture's structure as empty headed
  sections — in the sample, `MAPMAKING` with `INTRODUCTION`, `A BRIEF
  INTRODUCTION OF MAPMAKING`, `HISTORY OF MAPMAKING`, `PAPER TOWNS`,
  `CONCLUSION` — which the candidate fills in by hand while listening.

The note-taking half is worth more than the multiple-choice half.

## What the app covers

Counting only what is *directly* targeted — grammar knowledge helps
everywhere, and that is not what this table measures:

| Section | Points | At three topics | Now (8 topics) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cloze | 15 | ~3.75 (2 modal blanks, one causative) | **~10** — 7 of the 10 blanks have a lesson |
| Closest meaning | 15 | ~3 (modal perfects, passive reporting) | **~12** — very nearly the sample's own list |
| Reading | 21 | 0 | 0 |
| Paragraph completion | 9 | 0 | 0 |
| Listening | 20 | 0 | 0 |
| Writing | ~20 | 0 | 0 |
| **Total** | **~100** | **~7** | **~22** |

The `~7` column is the number this document shipped with, and it was
right when it was written: three topics, none of which the sample cloze
tests. Five more shipped on 2026-09-04 and it stopped being right without
anything being said, which is how a coverage claim usually goes wrong.

**The `Now` column is arithmetic over this document and the manifest, not
a second reading of the paper.** Blank by blank, from the §Cloze table
above: discourse markers (`connectors`), two modal blanks (`modals`), the
causative (`gerunds-infinitives`), comparatives (`closest-meaning`),
relative pronouns (`relative-clauses`) and quantifiers (`quantifiers`)
all have a lesson — seven blanks, ~10.5 points. The three left are the
two vocabulary items and `so / such`, and none of them is close to
shipping. For closest meaning, the sample turns on future perfect, third
conditional, passive reporting, modal perfects, `unless`, correlative
comparatives, `as … as`, `enough to` and concession; every one of those
now has a lesson, so the honest figure is "most of 15" and ~12 is the
conservative end of it.

`js/topics.js` holds the blank-by-blank map (`CLOZE_BLANKS`) so that
Profil can *derive* the covered count rather than repeat a number, and
`npm run validate` fails if one of those topic ids stops matching the
manifest. The point totals per section are still written down, because
those change only when the paper does.

The research arm estimated 12–19% before any paper was available. The
first reading of the real paper put it lower; eight topics put it above.

## What this changes

1. **The three topics are the wrong three.** Not wrong to have — they are
   real B1–B2 grammar and they carry two cloze blanks and part of the
   restatement section — but they were chosen without a paper in front of
   anyone. The next grammar to write is **discourse markers, relative
   clauses, conditionals, comparatives, quantifiers, `so/such`** and the
   causatives already half-covered.
2. **Every item on this paper is four-option multiple choice** except the
   note-taking sheet. The app's control is already the right control; what
   is missing is the *shapes* around it — a shared passage, a shared
   paragraph, a sentence to restate.
3. **Restatement is the cheapest real section to add.** It is one sentence
   and four options, with no passage and no new engine work. 15 points, a
   quarter of Session I.
4. **A cloze passage is not ten of our questions in a row.** One text,
   ten blanks, each testing something different, all options printed
   together. The unit is the passage.
5. **Reading questions cite numbered paragraphs**, so a passage in the
   content schema needs its paragraphs addressable, not just a blob of
   text.
6. **Listening cannot be faked with text-to-speech**, and the note-taking
   half — the larger half — is not a multiple-choice task at all. The
   research arm's recommendation to defer listening survives contact with
   the paper, and for a better reason than it gave.
