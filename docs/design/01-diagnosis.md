# Why the design rounds stopped working

2026-09-11. The owner asked the right question after rejecting a fourth
interface round: *what am I doing wrong as the person writing the
prompts, and why can't we reach the result I want? When we moved from
the first version to this design system we researched, examined and
learned in every area — colour, font, design, use — and the protocol
worked flawlessly. We can't do that now.*

He is right that it worked and right that it stopped. This file finds
out why, from this repository rather than from opinion, before any
design work restarts.

---

## 1 · Five measurements

**1. The research exists. It was not read.**

`docs/research/visual-longevity.md` is 1,201 lines, written for exactly
the question the last four rounds were trying to answer: how does this
app stay alive to look at without becoming a toy. Its §6 refuses, with
reasons: *streaks, XP, badges, mascots, confetti, skins, cosmetic
unlocks*. Its §0 computes that a per-topic accent is arithmetically
impossible — holding the accent's solved lightness and chroma, the gamut
leaves 148 usable degrees of hue, so ten topics would sit 15° apart and
be indistinguishable.

v0.62 shipped a streak with a flame, confetti at 80%, and a per-topic
hue generated by hashing the topic id. Three of that file's refusals, in
one release, six weeks after it was written.

**2. The rounds that failed cite no research at all.**

| plan | research files cited |
|---|---|
| `beta1-plan.md` | 2 |
| `ui2-plan.md` | **0** |
| `ui3-plan.md` | **0** |
| `app-plan.md` | 1 |

**3. The one question that keeps failing got the least study.**

Twenty-two research files, 20,504 lines, a 932-line average.
`docs/research/premium.md` — the file for "make it look professional" —
is **190 lines**. A fifth of the average, for the question that has now
consumed five releases.

**4. The levers were already ranked, and I worked the list backwards.**

`visual-longevity.md` §5 ranks them by payoff per unit of risk:

1. lesson shape following the material *(costs no CSS)*
2. richness earned from the learner's own record
3. an accent per **tier** (five, 35° apart, all measured and in gamut)
4. motion
5. one drawn mark per topic
6. — and §5.8, *consider subtracting*

The last four rounds did 4 and 5 repeatedly, invented a per-**topic**
accent the file had ruled out, and never touched 1 or 2. Lever 1 is a
measured finding sitting untouched: all sixty lessons are the same
shape; 21 distinct block sequences exist across 60 lessons and the two
commonest cover 40% of them; every lesson opens `text > contrast` and
closes on `decision`.

**5. Sixty-two releases, 237 commits, 62,127 lines of documentation.**
The project does not lack rigour. It lacks rigour *pointed at this
question*.

## 2 · What the protocol actually was, when it worked

Reconstructed from the history of the round the owner remembers as
flawless — the move to the current design system:

1. **A defect stated as a number.** Not "the type is ugly": 54% of
   rendered characters were at 13px and 11px in the dimmest grey in the
   palette, needing Lc 113 against a ceiling of 107.
2. **Research with a target.** `beta1-palette.md` (850 lines),
   `beta1-hierarchy.md` (800), `ui-improve.md` (726) — each ending in a
   recommendation and a list of what could not be verified.
3. **Two concrete alternatives, and a choice.** The first task of that
   round is still in the list: *"Adım 0: iki paletle mockup'lar"* —
   mockups in two palettes. He looked, he chose, and only then was
   anything built.
4. **The spec before the code.** The rules were written down with their
   reasons, then implemented.
5. **A measurement that outlives the argument.** `npm run color`, the
   `PAIRS` table, the sweep. Whether a token passes is not a matter of
   taste.

Every one of those five is missing from the last four rounds. The
protocol did not fail. It was abandoned, by me, one step at a time:

| the protocol | what the last four rounds did |
|---|---|
| defect as a number | a verdict as an adjective (*premium, ugly, slop*) |
| research with a target | a plan document written from first principles in one sitting |
| two alternatives, a choice | one finished synthesis, presented for approval |
| spec before code | spec written in the **last** phase, to justify the code (UI 2 phase 4, UI 3 phase 5) |
| measurement that settles it | measurement of legibility, which cannot settle beauty |

## 3 · The owner's half, stated as fixable inputs

These are not complaints. Each is a missing input with a cheap fix, and
the evidence for each is in this conversation's own record.

**3.1 There is no reference set, and it is the single largest gap.**
Across five rounds the owner has never named an app, a site, a page or a
screenshot he likes. Design practice begins with references for a
precise reason: taste does not survive translation into adjectives, and
it survives perfectly as examples. "Premium" is shared by Revolut and by
a Kinfolk spread, and they have nothing in common. Every round I have
been aiming at a target only he can see.

*The fix: five to ten screenshots of things he likes, plus one or two he
dislikes. Ten minutes of his time removes the largest source of error in
the project.*

**3.2 Verdicts carry one bit; observations carry the fix.** Sorted by
what actually happened:

| feedback | kind | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| "the reader's middle section is hard to read" | observation | fixed in one pass, accepted |
| "the back and forward buttons should be equal" | observation | fixed in one pass, accepted |
| "sub-headings in a different colour" | observation | fixed in one pass, accepted |
| "white mode is rubbish, use cream, measure it with more than one tool" | observation + criterion | fixed in one pass, accepted |
| "the exam countdown is onboarding-excess, cut it" | observation | clear, actionable |
| "be premium, be visionary, make it sparkle" | verdict | round rejected |
| "disgraceful, I hated it" | verdict | round rejected |
| "it became AI slop" | verdict | round rejected |
| "I couldn't like it" | verdict | round rejected |

His specific feedback has a perfect record. His adjectives have never
once produced an accepted round. That is not a comment on him — it is
information about which channel carries signal.

**3.3 "From scratch" has never been accompanied by releasing the
frame.** Every round opens with *sıfırdan* and keeps the same
non-negotiables: no build step, the measured palette, the type scale,
the two-tab navigation (marked *settled, do not reopen* in CLAUDE.md),
the content, the engine. A rebuild inside an unchanged frame is a
re-skin by construction. Naming which parts of the frame are genuinely
open — as he did for the tabs, once, and for onboarding — is what makes
"from scratch" mean something.

**3.4 Judgement arrives only on finished work.** By the time he sees a
round, two thousand lines exist and the only available verdict is yes or
no. Nothing cheaper has been put in front of him since the two-palette
mockups.

**3.5 One message carries four levels.** Product strategy, visual
taste, process instruction and frustration arrive together, and I
resolve the priority by guessing. Usually I optimise the loudest
sentence.

## 4 · My half, which is the larger one

1. **I stopped reading our own work.** §1 is the proof. Four rounds of
   design decisions taken without opening the 20,504 lines of research
   that already answered several of them.
2. **I wrote plans instead of studying.** `ui2-plan.md` and
   `ui3-plan.md` are confident, structured and sourced from nothing but
   my own reasoning in one sitting.
3. **I inverted the protocol** — built, then wrote the specification
   afterwards to justify what was built. A spec written after the code
   cannot constrain the code.
4. **I offered one synthesis instead of alternatives**, which turns
   every review into a referendum on my taste and throws away the one
   mechanism that worked.
5. **I converted a taste problem into an engineering problem** and
   reported green checks as success. 3,462 passing checks cannot tell
   anyone whether a screen is beautiful, and I used them as if they
   could. That is the mechanism that produces something technically
   correct and lifeless — which is precisely what he keeps naming.

## 5 · The options, cause → purpose → result

**A · Carry on: I synthesise, he judges.**
*Cause:* fastest per round, needs nothing from him.
*Purpose:* converge by iteration.
*Result:* measured — four rounds, four rejections. The channel carries
one bit per round and the target is invisible to me. **Refused.**

**B · Reference-led.** He supplies examples; I extract their invariants
as measurable properties.
*Cause:* taste is transmissible as examples and not as adjectives.
*Purpose:* replace *premium* with numbers — this type scale, this
contrast, this density, this radius, this motion duration.
*Result:* the target becomes external and checkable, which is the exact
property that made the palette round succeed. Costs him ten minutes.

**C · Choice-led.** Three deliberately different directions, one screen
each, fully realised; he picks.
*Cause:* this is literally step 0 of the round that worked.
*Purpose:* turn a refusal into a ranked choice, which also reveals
*which axis* he is judging on.
*Result:* direction settled in one round instead of four.

**D · Principle-led.** Derive everything from the repository's own
ranked research and ignore taste.
*Cause:* the research exists, is measured, and is being wasted.
*Purpose:* a defensible system.
*Result:* used alone, this is exactly UI 2 — correct and lifeless. It is
a constraint set, not a design.

**The path: B, then D, then C, in that order.** His references define
the target; the research defines what may not be spent; three directions
turn the remaining freedom into a choice he can make in thirty seconds.

Rows 5 onward were rewritten on 2026-09-11 after step 5 was rejected.
`06-sozel-yonerge.md` §0 says why: the round kept skipping the step
where the *material* is chosen, and went straight to screens built out
of a material nobody had agreed to.

Steps 1, 3 and 6 are mine. Step 2 needed ten minutes from him and got
three batches. Step 7 produces the choice; step 8 is where the craft
goes.

## 7 · The one input needed now

Five to ten screenshots or names of things he finds beautiful — apps,
sites, book pages, posters, anything — and one or two he finds ugly.
They do not need to be study apps. They do not need to be justified.

Without it, step 5 is three more guesses.
