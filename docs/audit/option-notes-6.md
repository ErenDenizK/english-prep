# `optionNotes` — Passive Voice

2026-09-04. Scope: `data/passive-voice/passive-voice.json` only.

**72 notes written** — three per item, on all 24 items, covering every wrong
option. No note on any key. Longest is 138 characters (`t9 / should be`);
most sit between 100 and 125, the shortest 88. `npm run format` and
`npm run check` clean: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## What the notes turn on

A passive item fails on one of three things, and each note names the one
that decides its own item:

- **the tense inside the passive** — which zaman the `be` is conjugated to
  (t1–t4, t16, and the `to have been` half of t19). The note says what
  period or moment the chosen form measures, and what the paragraph
  actually gives instead: a rutin, a kapanmış geçmiş an, a süre that runs
  to now, a tadilat that is still going.
- **whether the auxiliary is even assembled** — `be` missing, `been` where
  `be` belongs, `take` where `taken` belongs (t5–t8, t18, t20). These notes
  quote the lesson's rule in the lesson's words: *modalden sonra fiil daima
  yalın hâlde gelir*, *'been' yalnızca 'have' ile yaşar*, *'be supposed'
  tek başına kullanılmaz*.
- **whether the sentence wants a passive at all** — the active option that
  reverses the roles (t5–t8, t13–t15, t17, t19, t20, t22–t24). Here the
  note never says "aktif, o yüzden yanlış"; it plays the reversal back as a
  sentence: *romanı öğrencinin değil, öğrenciyi romanın yazdığını söyler*,
  *aşı bir araştırma ekibini geliştirmiş olur*, *çıkışların bir şeyi açık
  tuttuğunu söyler*.

Two categories are not about the passive's shape at all and their notes say
so: **Causative** (t13–t16) is about which verb takes `nesne + V3` and what
`make` / `let` / `do` / `take` would each mean instead, and **By + Agent**
(t21–t24) is about whether the agent carries information — *'someone' zaten
'bilmiyorum' demenin uzun hâlidir* — or whether there is an agent to name at
all (t23: an araç, not a fail).

Terminology is the lessons': `fail`, `araç`, `V3`, `yalın hâl`,
`modal + be + V3`, `yaptırma kalıbı`, `aktarım`. No second vocabulary was
introduced.

## Items where writing the notes showed the item is weak

**t1 — `has been baked` is defensible.** "Fresh bread has been baked every
morning at this bakery" is idiomatic English for a habit that runs up to
now, and the paragraph even supplies a duration ("three generations") —
attached to the recipe, not to the baking, which is the only thing keeping
the key clean. The note argues exactly that narrow point (the Present
Perfect wants the stretch it measures to be in the sentence, and üç kuşak
belongs to the tarif). It is the thinnest exclusion in the file. Attaching
the duration to the baking would break the item; attaching a clean
Present-Simple signal only to the baking would fix it.

**t16 — `had` is close to acceptable.** "By the time we moved in, the
previous owners had the entire kitchen renovated" is well formed and reads
naturally in speech; only the sequence-of-tenses preference after "by the
time" selects `had had`. The note argues the preference rather than a rule
breach, which is the honest thing to say and also the weakest thing to say.
This is the item most likely to be contested by a learner who is right.

**t9 and t12 — all three distractors are one distractor.** Every wrong
option is `modal + be`, i.e. the same single fault: no `have been`. They
differ only in which modal, so the notes had to be written on modal
*meaning* (kesinlik, beklenti, imkân) to avoid three copies of one
sentence. A learner who understands `have been` eliminates all three at
once, and the item measures one decision, not four.

**t17 and t20 — three distractors, one fault.** In t17 all three are
active (`is saying to`, `says to`, `is telling to`); in t20 two are active
and the third drops `to`. Again the notes carry the differentiation the
options do not.

**t5 — `should submit` and `should be submitting` are one distractor.**
Both are active. The note for the second leans on the belief that makes it
*look* different (the `be` inside `be submitting` reads as passive), which
is a real trap, but the fault is identical to the first option's.

**t21 — `by someone` and `by a person` are one distractor.** Both fail
because an indefinite agent adds nothing; `by a stranger` is the only one
that fails differently (it asserts something the paragraph denies). Two of
the three exclusions are the same exclusion.

**t22 and t24 — the perfect adds nothing.** `wrote` / `has written` and
`developed` / `has developed` fail on the same role reversal; the tense
difference between the pair is never load-bearing. The notes distinguish
the *belief* (past fact vs. achievement worth crediting now), not the
fault.

**Category shape, Passive with Modals (t5–t8).** All four items are built
identically: key = `modal + be + V3`, one active distractor, two
form breakages (`been` for `be`, or a missing V3). Across four items the
learner meets the same three tests four times with a different modal. The
category spec would show this immediately; it does not show inside any one
item.

**Category shape, Causative (t13–t15).** Three consecutive items whose
distractor sets are `make` / `do` / `take` (t13, t14) and `do` / `make` /
`let` (t15) — the same rejections three times over. t16 is the only item
in the category that tests the conjugation of the pattern rather than the
choice of verb, and it is also the only one with a contestable option
(above). Writing nine notes for three items required inventing nine
different angles on two rules.

## Explanations that leave a distractor unnamed

Twenty-one of the twenty-four items name at most two of their three wrong
options in `explanation` + `tip`; the notes now cover the gap. Three items
leave **two** options unmentioned and have the shortest explanations in the
file — **t4** (125 chars; `was`, `has been`), **t16** (168; `have had`,
`did have`), **t20** (126; `is supposing to`, `supposes to`). t23 (206) is
close behind, naming two options but never `wrote`. If any item in this
topic is revisited for its explanation rather than its options, these four
are the ones where the explanation is doing least.

No explanation was found to claim something the paragraph does not support.

## Wording conventions followed

English forms are quoted with straight single quotes, matching every
`explanation` and `tip` in this file. No `**bold**` was used: the notes are
one sentence each and the emphasis would have fallen on the English form,
which the quotes already mark. No note restates the key, compares options,
or refers to anything the learner has not seen — each is written to the one
person who picked that option.
