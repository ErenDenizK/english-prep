# `optionNotes` — Gerunds & Infinitives

2026-09-04. Scope: `data/gerunds-infinitives/gerunds-infinitives.json` only.

**72 notes written** — three per item, on all 24 items, covering every wrong
option. No note on any key. Longest is 152 characters, most sit between 100
and 140. `npm run format && npm run check` clean.

## What the notes say, by category

- **Verb + Gerund vs Verb + Infinitive** (t1–t4) and **Adjective + Infinitive
  vs Preposition + Gerund** (t13–t16): the note names the governing verb or
  adjective and what it takes (`'avoid' -ing ister`, `'reluctant' edat almaz`),
  or, where the option is grammatical, says which reading of the paragraph it
  imposes. Both categories have items whose closest distractor is well formed
  (`refuses to drive`, `enjoyed repairing`, `eager to hand`, `slow to point`),
  and those notes argue meaning, not form.
- **Both, With a Meaning Change** (t5–t8): no note claims a grammar fault.
  Each says where the option puts the second action relative to the main verb
  — before it (an ended activity, a remembered act, a method actually tried)
  or after it (a task not yet done, the reason for a pause, news being
  delivered now) — in the lesson's own terms.
- **Causative** (t9–t12): the note names the pattern the option breaks —
  `make`/`let`/`have` + bare infinitive, `get` + `to`, `have + eşya + V3`, or
  the object-before-verb order in t11 — and adds the role mismatch where the
  option is well formed (`let him go`, `made him drive`).
- **Purpose vs Reason** (t17–t20) and **Used To** (t21–t24): the note names
  whether a preposition precedes the slot, whether the `for` is licensed by a
  verb or noun in the sentence, and whether the subject is something that can
  become accustomed to anything at all.

## Items where a distractor is only weakly excluded

Two, both in *Both, With a Meaning Change*. In neither case was a note
impossible — the paragraph does select the key — but the exclusion rests on
reading the framing rather than on anything the option itself gets wrong.

- **t7, `stopped to use`.** "The technician stopped to use the socket on the
  other wall, just to see whether it made any difference" is not ill formed
  and is not absurd; it is only unmotivated, because nothing in the paragraph
  is being interrupted. The note says exactly that. If the item is ever
  revised, giving the technician an activity he breaks off would sharpen it,
  or dropping the option.
- **t6, `tried filling`.** Excluded because a trial method needs a problem to
  solve, and the paragraph gives none — they simply reach a fountain. Softer
  than the item's other two distractors, both of which fail outright.

Everything else has a hard reason. In seven items — **t3, t9, t14, t15, t17,
t19, t21** — every wrong option is ill formed and the key is the only
grammatical choice; those notes name the specific fault (the verb that governs
the slot, the preposition, the adjective's complement), never "yanlış". The
briefing carried eight from an earlier review; counting the options myself I
get seven, with t12 the near miss (two of its three distractors are ill formed,
`made him drive` being well formed and wrong on role).

## Contradictions with existing `explanation` / `tip`

None found. Three places worth recording where the note had to go beyond what
the item already says:

- **t13** — the `explanation` argues `eager to hand` and `reluctant of
  handing` but never mentions `eager for handing`. The note covers it on both
  counts: `eager` takes no preposition, and the meaning is inverted anyway.
- **t9 / t12** — the `tip`s cover `make`, `let` and `get` but not `have`, and
  each item has a `had ... to ...` distractor. The notes state the `have +
  kişi + yalın fiil` pattern, which is in the lesson's `forms` block and its
  third `pitfall`.
- **t11** — the `explanation` calls `had reprinted them` "present perfect
  sanılarak" kurulmuş. The note avoids naming a tense and says only that the
  pattern puts the object before the verb, which is the fault the learner can
  act on and is what the lesson's `decision` rule says.

## Wording conventions followed

English forms are quoted with single quotes, matching every `explanation` and
`tip` in this file; backticks are literal in this app's renderer. No note
asserts that a verb "never" takes a form except where the lesson itself states
the rule (`edattan sonra yalın fiil gelmez`).
