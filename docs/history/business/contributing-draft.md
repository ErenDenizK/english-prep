# A contributor note, ready to use

`licensing.md` §5 is the only item in `docs/business/` with a near
deadline, and the deadline is *before a second person writes anything
into `data/`*. This file exists so that acting on it is a one-minute
decision rather than a task.

**Not legal advice, and deliberately not lawyer-shaped.** The point of a
note like this at two people's scale is not to survive a court. It is
that both people wrote down what they thought they agreed, on the day
they agreed it, so that a decision two years from now — sell it, give it
away, put it behind a store, hand it to a school — is one conversation
instead of an archaeology project.

---

## Why this is needed at all

Whoever writes a lesson or a question owns the copyright in what they
wrote, by default, from the moment they write it. Not the person who owns
the repository and not the person who asked for it. So without a note,
any later decision about the corpus needs the individual agreement of
every person who contributed to it — and the moment to ask is while it
is a friendly sentence, not after it has become a negotiation.

This is not about distrust. It is exactly the same reason the app writes
down why a colour was chosen: the person who has to act on it later is
someone with no memory of the conversation, and that person is usually
one of the two of you.

## Option A — a paragraph in the repository

If a `CONTRIBUTING.md` is added, this is the whole of what it needs to
say. Everything else about how to contribute is already in
`docs/agents/README.md` and `docs/CONTENT_GUIDE.md`.

> ## Contributing
>
> Content and code here are written to the standards in
> `docs/agents/` and `docs/CONTENT_GUIDE.md`; start there.
>
> **On ownership.** By contributing content or code to this repository
> you agree that your contribution is licensed to the project under the
> same terms as the rest of it, and that the project owner may
> relicense it — including under a commercial licence — as part of
> relicensing the project as a whole. You keep the copyright in what you
> wrote and may reuse it elsewhere yourself. This is here so that a
> later decision about the project does not require tracking down
> everyone who ever contributed to it.

## Option B — a message, which is probably the right one

At two friends' scale, a message that both people can find again is
worth as much as a file, and it is far more likely to actually be read.
Turkish, because the person reading it is Turkish and the point is that
they understand it:

> Bu projeye ders veya soru yazacaksan, baştan bir şeyi yazılı hâle
> getirelim ki ileride konuşmak zorunda kalmayalım:
>
> Yazdığın şeyin telif hakkı sende kalır, istediğin yerde kendin de
> kullanabilirsin. Ama projeye kattığın kısmı projenin geri kalanıyla
> aynı şartlarda kullanma ve — projeyi bir bütün olarak lisanslarsak,
> ticari bir lisans da dâhil — yeniden lisanslama hakkını bana vermiş
> oluyorsun.
>
> Bunu güvensizlikten değil, tam tersi için yazıyorum: bir yıl sonra
> "bunu satalım mı, ücretsiz mi bıraksalım, okula mı verelim" diye
> konuşurken, o konuşmanın önünde bir engel olmasın diye. Şu an bir
> cümle; sonra bir mesele oluyor.
>
> Uyuyorsan da olur, sen okuyunca "tamam" de yeter.

Then keep the reply. A screenshot in the repository or a line in
`CONTRIBUTING.md` saying who agreed and when is enough.

---

## What this does not settle, on purpose

- **How the money is split, if there ever is any.** That is a different
  conversation and it should not be smuggled into this one. Copyright
  and revenue share are separate questions and mixing them makes the
  easy one feel like the hard one.
- **Whose name is on it.** Also separate, also worth saying out loud
  rather than defaulting.
- **Whether an *adi ortaklık* costs the Turkish tax exemption**
  (`two-apps.md` §7.2). That one is for an SMMM, and it should be asked
  *before* a developer account is opened in anyone's name — because the
  answer might decide whose name it goes in.

Nothing has been added to the repository. Whether to use Option A,
Option B, both or neither is the owner's call, and so is the wording.
