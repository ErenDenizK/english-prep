## 2 · The two repaired categories

### 2.1 Significance & Priority — t15, and what the rewrite cost

**The charge.** `RE-AUDIT.md` §3.1: t15 as the *first* repair left it
reproduced `pitfall` b8 — *[small word] · contrastive · it changed the
outcome*, same two candidate words, same slot — and b8 is the block
immediately above the `check` at b9 that draws from this category. §4 added
that `looked ____ ; even so` left `negligible` defensible.

**Is it gone.** Yes. The shipped paragraph is

> Fitting the whole fleet with the new tyres cut fuel use by four tenths of
> one per cent. The depot's auditors call that a **____** saving: anything
> the fuel meters can separate from ordinary week-to-week variation has to
> be carried in the annual return, so the four tenths are entered in full
> rather than rounded away.

b8 is unchanged (*The effect was negligible, but it changed the outcome.* →
*marginal*). The concessive is gone, nothing in the paragraph depends on
the four tenths, the slot moved from bare predicative to attributive, and
the deciding evidence is now a reporting rule rather than a consequence.
The two sentences share no frame and no scenario. **Charge cleared.**

**The three wrong options, substituted.** The test is
`question-author.md` rule 2 — an option a competent teacher would *accept*
is a wrong option, not a less natural one.

| option | the sentence it produces | my verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `negligible` | *"The depot's auditors call that a negligible saving: … so the four tenths are entered in full rather than rounded away."* | **Not defensible.** `negligible` means *small enough to be left out*; the clause the colon introduces says the figure is not left out, and *rounded away* names the precise act the word licenses. To rescue it a reader has to supply a concession (*negligible, yet the rules force it in*) that the text does not contain — and *so* makes the clause a consequence, not a concession. The old paragraph supplied that concession with *even so*, which is exactly what §4 caught; the new one does not. |
| `substantial` | *"…call that a substantial saving: anything the fuel meters can separate from ordinary week-to-week variation has to be carried…"* | **Not defensible.** Four tenths of one per cent, and the reason clause is about a figure that has to be *rescued* from rounding. Nothing needs a rule to keep a substantial saving in the books. |
| `considerable` | *"…call that a considerable saving: …"* | **Not defensible.** Same size objection, and the lesson's own gloss puts `considerable` on unmeasured abstract degrees (*interest, difficulty*), the opposite of a metered fuel figure. |

None is dead (rule 3): all four are ordinary adjectives before `saving`,
all four are grammatical, and each fails on an axis the lesson teaches.
The blank's neighbours (*call that a ____ saving*) decide nothing; the size
is in sentence one and the countability in the reason clause, which is rule
4 satisfied.

I reached `marginal` on this paragraph blind, before reading any of the
above (§0.1). `negligible` did not present itself as a candidate.

**What the fix cost, and the one thing that survives it.** The log's own
"honest cost" — losing the seven-per-cent comparison flattens the size
axis — is real and I agree with the trade.

The residual I would put in front of the supervisor is different and the
log does not name it: **b8's `why` and t15's key now share a
discriminator, phrased in the same metaphor**. b8 says *sonucu değiştiren
bir etki hesaptan çıkarılamaz. Küçük ama gerçek etki marginal'dır*; t15
turns on *entered in full rather than rounded away*. A learner served t15
at the b9 check has read the rule that decides it one block above, in the
language of accounts.

I do **not** treat that as blocking, for a reason I want on the record
because it is the standard the whole corpus is being judged by: **that is
what a `check` is.** `CONTENT_GUIDE.md` places checks "where the learner
has just been given something worth trying", and the rule
`question-author.md` states is about the *sentence and the scenario*, not
the rule. The corpus already ships items in this shape and the last pass
certified them: `Scale` `pitfall` b7 (*Sales stayed under the margin.* →
`threshold`) demonstrates precisely the discrimination t4 tests, two blocks
above the same check, and `RE-AUDIT.md` passes `Scale` without comment. If
b8↔t15 is a defect, so is b7↔t4, and so is half the topic. What was
blocking in §3.1 was the *sentence shape* being reproduced, and it is gone.

### 2.2 Stance & Disposition — `pitfall` b8, and its whole lesson

reluctant.* → *deliberate*, which marks as an error the transferred epithet

**Is it gone.** Yes. The shipped block is

> **wrong:** *The board wanted the merger and was reluctant, so it asked
> for a second legal opinion.*
> **right:** *The board wanted the merger and was cautious, so it asked for

Its subject is a party, not a product, so the transfer is neither used nor
denied anywhere in the block. **Charge cleared.**

project's worst historical failure. I ran all twelve blocks, not the log's

**Leak test.** A `check` at b9 is filled from this category, so I checked
what b8 hands the learner. `cautious` is the one word of the five that is
**never a key** (t21 `reluctant`, t22 `deliberate`, t23 `persistent`, t24
`indifferent`) — I verified this against the file, not the log. The
previous b8 taught `deliberate`, which *is* t22's key. So the replacement
strictly reduces what a check can leak.

Better than neutral, it **helps two items by the rule rather than by the
item**: b8's ground for rejecting `reluctant` is *the party wants the
thing*, which is exactly why `reluctant` fails at t23 (the widow wants it
and keeps asking) — and t23's own tip says the same thing in the same words
(*'reluctant' tek başına kullanıldığında 'gönülsüz', yani tam tersidir*).
And b8's `cautious` requires an avoided risk, which is how t21 excludes its
nearest distractor.

No frame is reproduced: b8 is *X wanted Y and was ____, so …*; t21 is
attributive, t22 is *had been ____ , not careless*, t23 is *but she was
____ , and six years on*, t24 is *it was simply ____*.
