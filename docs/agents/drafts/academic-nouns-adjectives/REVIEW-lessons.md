# Lesson review — `academic-nouns-adjectives`

Sufficiency pass, six lessons against their own 24 questions. **Not blind:**
this pass reads the keys, because the question it asks — *does the lesson
contain what its category's items require* — cannot be asked without them. The
blind item pass is running separately; nothing here is an agreement rate.

**Method.** Each lesson's `decision` block was run as a literal checklist over
its own category's four items: which rule fires, on which word, and whether
anything else in the lesson eliminates the key. Every English string in a
lesson was then compared against every paragraph in its category.

**Verdicts.**

| Category | Verdict | Blocking defect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scale & Extent | ships | — |
| Evidence & Inference | ships | — |
| Certainty & Doubt | **does not ship** | The `decision` block certifies a distractor at t9 and at t10, and reaches neither key |
| Significance & Priority | ships | — |
| Constraint & Requirement | ships | — |
| Stance & Disposition | **does not ship** | The `forms` block's positional column eliminates the key and licenses a distractor at t21 and t22 |

Two topic-level findings below are as serious as either: **eight numeric
contradictions between explanations and their own paragraphs**, and **six
`examples` blocks that are verbatim copies of the `contrast` examples above
them**, which is what drives this topic's giveaway scores.

## 1 · Scale & Extent (extent / scope / magnitude / margin / threshold) — ships

R4→t1, R3→t2, R1→t3, R2→t4. All four keys reached; no rule fires on a
distractor.

### Worth fixing · untrue claim · `magnitude` does not mean "unusually large"

`contrast` b1: *"magnitude :: Büyüklüğün kendisi, üstelik **alışılmışın
dışında** büyük."* R4 inherits it: *"Cümle boyutu **alışılmışın dışında büyük**
diye niteliyorsa … => magnitude"*.

`magnitude` is a neutral scale word. *The magnitude of the effect was small*,
*an earthquake of magnitude 3.1*, *estimates differ by an order of magnitude* —
all standard, none large. Its association with size comes from what people
usually measure, not from its meaning.

**Correction:** *magnitude* = size expressed on a scale, typically of a single
event, force or quantity. The genuine contrast with `extent` is *scale of one
thing* versus *how far something reaches or spreads*, and it survives without
the "unusually large" clause — which is doing no work in this corpus anyway,
since t1's cue is the scale itself (*"twice the speed the deck had ever been
tested for"*).

### Worth fixing · the lesson makes a promise its own item breaks

`text` b10: *"cümle bir şeyin yalnızca büyük olduğunu söylüyorsa **extent** de
**magnitude** de savunulur, ve **iyi bir soru seni bu ikisi arasında seçim
yapmaya zorlamaz**."* R6 says the same as a rule.

t1 keys `magnitude` with `extent` on the paper, and its own explanation names
`extent` as the closest wrong option. R4 does resolve it, so this is not
blocking — but the reassurance is false about this lesson's own four items, and
a learner who has believed b10 will not look for the discriminator.

### Worth fixing · untrue claim · `pitfall` b6's `wrong` is acceptable English

```
WRONG: The report explains the scope of the damage.
RIGHT: The report explains the extent of the damage.
```

*Extent of the damage* is the stronger collocation; *scope of the damage* is
current English and a teacher would accept it. The pitfall marks acceptable
English as an error to make the extent/scope line look sharper than it is.

### Worth fixing · giveaway, the worst single count in either topic

*below … threshold* appears **four times** in this lesson:

- `contrast` b2 example — *"Nothing changes below this threshold."*
- `forms` b3 example — *"below the threshold"*
- `examples` b5 — *"Nothing changes below this threshold."* (a verbatim repeat)
- `pitfall` b7 `right` — *"The temperature stayed below the threshold."*

t4's blank is *"falls **below** a certain ____"*, and t4 is served as a `check`
inside this lesson. Key plus deciding collocate, four exposures, two of them
identical sentences.

### Worth fixing · L5 · `pitfall` b8 is untested

*to a large extent* vs *to a large scope*. `extent` is never keyed, and the
pattern appears in no item.

### Note · role

`extent` is an option in **4/4** items and keyed in none.

## 2 · Evidence & Inference (evidence / indication / assumption / implication / consensus) — ships

R4→t5, R2→t6, R3→t7, R1→t8. All four reached, none misfiring.

This lesson has the best complementation coverage in either topic — `evidence
for/of`, `indication of/that`, `on the assumption that`, `implications for`,
`consensus on/among`. Two of the four words `exam-vocabulary.md` §3.3 names as
missing (`implication for`, `consensus on`) are taught here, correctly, in a
`forms` block.

### Worth fixing · L5 · an entire decision rule fires on nothing

R6: *"Seçtiğini boşluğa koyup denetle: **boşluk an alıyorsa ya da çoğulsa**,
sayılamayan üyeler o biçime girmez"*, backed by `pitfall` b6 (*"He gave **an**
evidence"*) and the `forms` countability column.

No blank in this category takes `an` or is plural:

| item | frame |
| --- | --- |
| t5 | *the ____ needed to show* |
| t6 | *on the ____ that every student* |
| t7 | *not the finding itself but its ____* |
| t8 | *the ____ among excavators* |

One of six decision rules, one of two pitfalls and one `forms` column are spent
on a check the corpus never invokes. This is the L5 shape exactly — and it is
the cheapest to fix from the question side, since one item with *"an ____ that
the stocks were thinning"* would use all three at once.

### Worth fixing · giveaways · three of the corpus's worst five

- **t8** — the item's own explanation says the deciding cue is
  *"**among** excavators"*. The lesson prints *consensus among economists*
  **three times**: `contrast` b2 example, `forms` b3 example, `examples` b5.
  Same key, same governing preposition, same frame, one noun changed. A learner
  who meets t8 as a `check` in this lesson has read the answer three blocks up.
- **t7** — *"not the **finding** itself but its ____"* against *"The **finding**
  has implications for future work."*, printed twice (b2 and b5).
- **t6** — built on the lesson's own frame. `forms` gives *on the assumption
  that costs fall*; `pitfall` b7 `right` is *"We started from the assumption
  that costs **would** fall."*; t6's paragraph is *"was built **on the** ____
  **that** every student **would** reach the campus"*.

### Note · role

`indication` is an option in **4/4** items and keyed in none.

## 3 · Certainty & Doubt (apparent / plausible / questionable / conclusive / tentative) — does not ship

Run as a literal checklist, the `decision` block gets two of the four items
wrong, and both failures point at a real hole in the teaching rather than at a
wording slip.

### Blocking · the procedure certifies `questionable` at `academic-nouns-adjectives-t9`

t9 keys `conclusive` in a negative frame: *"what the prosecution finally laid
before the court was suggestive but **never** ____ ."*

- R2 fires only *"Cümle konunun kapandığını söylüyorsa: kanıt tartışmayı
  bitiriyor, geriye şüphe kalmıyorsa"*. This sentence says the opposite, so R2
  does not fire on the key. The `decision` block says nothing about negated
  frames anywhere; the polarity flip is modelled once, in an `examples` note
  (*"The tests are not conclusive."*), and never as a rule.
- R4 does fire: *"Cümle **bir kusur**, bir itiraz ya da yönteme yönelik bir
  şüphe taşıyorsa => questionable"*. The paragraph carries objections — two
  witnesses against a third, tyre casts fitting *"a model sold in the
  thousands"*. `questionable` is on the paper.

The discriminator the item needs — *insufficient* evidence versus
*untrustworthy* evidence — does exist in the lesson, but only in `text` b10:
*"questionable'ı seçmek için cümlenin **kaynağa ya da yönteme** kuşku
düşürmesi gerekir."* R4 omits it, and R4 is the block a learner carries into
the exam. This is the `decision` block being looser than the lesson's own prose,
which is the failure `CONTENT_GUIDE.md` names when it says a signal on both
sides is worse than no signal.

### Blocking · the procedure certifies `apparent` at `academic-nouns-adjectives-t10`

t10 keys `plausible`. R5 fires — the paragraph finds the explanation credible
without proving it.

So does R1: *"Cümle görüneni gerçekle karşı karşıya getiriyorsa: söylenen şey
sonra yalanlanıyor, açıklanıyor **ya da bir kuşkuyla dengeleniyorsa** =>
apparent"*. t10 is precisely a claim balanced by a doubt — *"nothing in the
surviving papers contradicts it — **yet** the register itself stops in 1699, so
the question can never be closed"*. `apparent` is on the paper.

Two rules fire, name different words, and the block gives no ordering between
them. The only thing that breaks the tie is a positional note in a different
block (`contrast` b1: *"apparent (seeming) çoğunlukla isimden önce gelir"*),
and the `decision` block never sends the learner there. R1's third disjunct is
broader than its own head clause and should not be a disjunct at all.

### Blocking-adjacent · L5, the largest in the corpus

`text` b0 declares the lesson's centre: *"**Asıl tuzak apparent'tadır.**"* The
lesson then gives `apparent`:

- a whole `contrast` block (b1), both senses;
- two of the six `forms` rows;
- two of the six `examples` items;
- **two of the six `decision` rules** (R1 and R6).

And no item in the category is keyed on `apparent`, or turns on which of its two
senses is meant. `apparent` is a distractor in **4/4** items. The single thing
the lesson says is the real trap is the single thing its own questions never
spring. This is the *Present Perfect vs Past Simple* defect, at the largest
scale this pipeline has found it.

### Blocking-adjacent · `optionNotes` · the `apparent` notes are not notes

Kickoff rule 1: every wrong option is a different word, so each note must
distinguish *that* word. The four `apparent` notes do not:

- t9 — *"Ya 'açıkça görülen' ya da 'görünüşte öyle olan' demektir; cümle
  hangisi olduğunu söylemez ve **ikisi de buraya oturmaz**."*
- t10 — *"İki anlamı var … Cümle hangisi olduğunu söylemiyor, **ikisi de
  oturmuyor**."*
- t11 — *"Denetçilerin itirazı sonuçların görünürlüğüyle ilgili değil."*
- t12 — *"İki anlamı da raporun sayısının neden değişeceğini açıklamıyor."*

Three of the four give the meaning and then *assert* that it does not fit. A
learner who chose `apparent` is told their word is ambiguous and that both
readings fail — which is a verdict, not a reason. The `plausible`, `tentative`
and `questionable` notes in the same four items do the job properly, so the
standard is met everywhere except on the word the lesson calls the main trap.

### Worth fixing · giveaway

t10's *"Fever is ____ as an **explanation**"* against *"That is one plausible
**explanation**."* — `contrast` b3 example, `forms` b4 example (*a plausible
explanation*), `examples` b6. Key plus deciding collocate, three exposures.

### Note

The `forms` positional column is hedged here (*"Genelde isimden önce"*), so
t12's predicative `tentative` and t9's predicative `conclusive` are not
contradicted the way §6's are. Keep the hedges.

## 4 · Significance & Priority (crucial / substantial / marginal / negligible / considerable) — ships

R1→t13, R4→t14, R3→t15, R2→t16. All four reached. The two-axis framing
(quantity vs consequence) is the best piece of teaching in this topic, and t13
tests it exactly — a tiny quantity that is `crucial`.

### Worth fixing · L5 · the pair the lesson works hardest on is never on one paper

`pitfall` b8 is *"The effect was **negligible**, but it changed the outcome"* →
`marginal`. Small-but-real versus not-worth-counting is the finest distinction
in the set.

`marginal` and `negligible` **never appear in the same option set**:

| item | key | options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| t13 | crucial | substantial, considerable, **negligible** |
| t14 | substantial | crucial, **marginal**, **negligible** |
| t15 | marginal | crucial, substantial, considerable |
| t16 | negligible | crucial, substantial, considerable |

t14 has both, but the key there is `substantial` and neither is live. So the
pair is offered as a real choice nowhere.

### Worth fixing · L5 · substantial vs considerable is likewise untested

The lesson admits they are near-synonyms (*"neredeyse eş anlamlıdır"*), spends
R4, R5 and R6 separating them, and no item requires the choice: t14 keys
`substantial` without `considerable` on the paper, and `considerable` is never
keyed anywhere.

### Worth fixing · `considerable` is taught by its company, not by a meaning

*"considerable :: Büyük miktar; **substantial ile neredeyse eş anlamlıdır**.
Eğilimi soyut derecelerdedir: ilgi, güçlük, etki."* That is a comparison and a
tendency, not a definition — and since `considerable` is never keyed, nothing
tests whether it is enough. It is the one member of sixty that the lesson does
not independently gloss.

### Worth fixing · giveaways

- t15 — *"the tyre **gain** looked ____"* against *"The **gain** was
  marginal."* (`contrast` b2, `examples` b5) and *"a marginal gain"* (`forms`
  b3). Three exposures of key plus collocate.
- t16 — *"the **difference** came to ninety lira … ____ against a budget"*
  against *"The **difference** is negligible."*, printed twice.
- t14 — *"a ____ sum"* against *"There was a substantial rise in **costs**."*,
  printed twice, with *costs* in the paragraph.

### Note · draft checker

`npm run draft` warns that t13 and t16 offer an identical option set.

### See also

Two of this category's four items misstate their own paragraph's numbers — the
one cue these items turn on. Topic-level section A.

## 5 · Constraint & Requirement (constraint / obligation / provision / criterion / exemption) — ships

R6→t17, R5→t18, R2→t19, R1→t20. All four reached.

**Credit where it is due.** This is the only category in either topic where a
taught complementation pattern actually decides an item. t18's blank is *"the
____ **to go** to the help of anyone in danger at sea"*, and the `forms` block
teaches `an obligation to + V` against `constraints on + N` and `an exemption
from + N`. `constraint on` — named by `exam-vocabulary.md` §3.3 as a gap — is
taught here and is load-bearing. The preposition hole §3.3 predicted is not
present in these lessons.

### Worth fixing · L5 · `provision` carries a third of the lesson and is never keyed

`provision` gets two of the six `decision` rules (R3 for the clause sense, R4
for the *provision of* sense), two `forms` rows and two `examples` items — and
it is the un-keyed fifth member, a distractor in t17, t18 and t20. Its second
sense (*"The provision of housing improved"*) is not tested at all, and R4 fires
on nothing.

### Worth fixing · L5 ×2 · two pitfalls built on words the category never offers

- b6 (`criterion` / `criteria`). `criteria` appears in no option. t19's frame
  (*"only one ____"*) requires the singular, so the trap is adjacent but never
  sprung.
- b8 (`exception` / `exemption`). `exception` is not a set member and appears
  in no option.

### Note

The t18 gain has a cost: if the `to + V` frame decides the item, the lexical
discrimination between `obligation` and `constraint` is not what is being
measured there. That is the cue pass's call, not a lesson defect — flagged so
the two passes do not each assume the other saw it.

## 6 · Stance & Disposition (reluctant / deliberate / cautious / persistent / indifferent) — does not ship

The meaning rules are sound: R5→t21, R1→t22, R2→t23, R3→t24, all four reached,
none firing on a distractor. The lesson fails on its other device.

### Blocking · the `forms` block eliminates the key and licenses a distractor, twice

`forms` b3 is written as a positional filter, one slot per adjective:

| form | use | pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reluctant | Mastarla | `be reluctant to + V` |
| cautious | Edatla | `be cautious about + N` |
| indifferent | Edatla | `be indifferent to + N` |
| deliberate | **İsimden önce; eylemi niteler** | `a deliberate + N` |
| persistent | **İsimden önce**; kişi ya da sorun | `a persistent + N` |

- **t21** — the blank is attributive: *"a ____ **signature**, but a
  signature."* The only option that table licenses in that slot is
  `deliberate` — *"İsimden önce; **eylemi niteler**"*, and a signature is an
  act. The key, `reluctant`, is licensed only in `be reluctant to + V`, and
  `pitfall` b6 spends itself reinforcing that frame. A learner filtering by the
  table strikes out the key and takes the distractor. The item additionally
  needs a transferred epithet — the signature is not reluctant, the signer is —
  which the lesson never mentions.
- **t22** — the blank is predicative with a preposition: *"the clerks had been
  ____ **about** it"*. In that table `about` belongs to `cautious`, which is an
  option; the key, `deliberate`, is listed as attributive only.

So in half the category the structural block points at a wrong answer while the
`decision` block points at the right one, and nothing in the lesson says which
device wins. Unlike §3's hedged table (*"Genelde isimden önce"*), these rows
carry no hedge.

The lesson contradicts itself on the same page, which is how to see that the
rows are wrong rather than merely incomplete: its own `contrast` examples one
block above are *"The omission **was** deliberate."* and *"The problem proved
**persistent**."* — both predicative, both from the two rows that say *isimden
önce*.

### Worth fixing · L5 · `reluctant to` is taught and never tested

`exam-vocabulary.md` §3.3 names `reluctant to` as a target pattern. The lesson
teaches it in `forms` and again in `pitfall` b6 (*"reluctant **of** signing"* →
*"reluctant **to** sign"*). No blank in this category is followed by `to + V`:
t21 *"a ____ signature"*, t22 *"been ____ about it"*, t23 *"she was ____"*, t24
*"it was simply ____"*. The one item keyed on `reluctant` uses the one frame the
lesson does not license.

### Worth fixing · giveaways

- `pitfall` b6 — *"She was reluctant **to sign** the form."* — against t21,
  keyed `reluctant`, blank *"a ____ **signature**"*.
- `pitfall` b7 `right` — *"After **years** of persistent effort, the team
  succeeded."* — against t23, keyed `persistent`, a paragraph built on refusals
  over **years**.

### See also

t23's explanation and two of its notes say *"on bir yıl"* where the paragraph
says *"six years on"*. Topic-level section A.

## Topic-level findings

### A · Blocking · eight numeric contradictions, in four items, all the same phrase

Explanations and `optionNotes` state numbers their own paragraph does not
contain. Every instance is the string **`on bir`** (eleven), and every instance
is wrong:

| item | paragraph says | explanation/note says | where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| t3 | *nine votes apart out of nineteen thousand* | *"on dokuz binde **on bir** oy"* | explanation |
| t15 | *seven per cent* | *"**yüzde on bir**lik eğitim tasarrufu"* | explanation; `considerable` note (*"yüzde on birin yanında"*) |
| t16 | *ninety lira across the entire print run* | *"**on bir lira**"* | explanation; `substantial` note |
| t23 | *six years on* | *"**on bir yıl** sonra"* | explanation; `reluctant` note; `indifferent` note |

Eight occurrences. Whatever produced them, it is systematic, not four
independent slips.

This matters more than a typo would: three of the four are `Significance &
Priority` and `Stance & Disposition` items whose *entire* selection mechanism is
the number, so the explanation contradicts the cue it is explaining. And the
blind pass **cannot** catch these — `tools/blind-corpus.mjs` withholds
`explanation` and `optionNotes` by allow-list, so this review is the only pass
that sees them.

### B · Blocking-adjacent · every `examples` block is a copy of the `contrast` examples

Measured across both topics:

| topic | `examples` sentences | identical to a `contrast` example in the same lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `academic-verbs` | 32 | **0** |
| `academic-nouns-adjectives` | 32 | **31** |

All six lessons here do it. `Certainty & Doubt`'s six-item `examples` block is
its `contrast` examples, in order, unchanged; so are the other five.

Two consequences, and the second is why this is not cosmetic:

1. One of the lesson's 6–14 blocks is spent repeating what the reader saw two
   blocks earlier. `CONTENT_GUIDE.md` asks `examples` for *"3–6 clean, isolated
   English sentences … Cover every side of the contrast"* — sentences that show
   the contrast *doing its work*, which is a different job from the one-line
   example inside the contrast.
2. It **triples** the exposure of the exact collocate the item is keyed on.
   With the `forms` example column usually printing the same collocation a
   third time, *"consensus among economists"*, *"below this threshold"*, *"The
   gain was marginal."* and *"That is one plausible explanation."* each reach
   the learner three times before they meet the `check` that turns on that
   collocate.

### C · Giveaway ranking, measured

Lesson strings sharing an item's **key** *and* a content word with that item's
paragraph, counted per item:

| item | key | count | shared word |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| t4 | threshold | **4** | *below* |
| t8 | consensus | **3** | *among* |
| t10 | plausible | **3** | *explanation* |
| t15 | marginal | **3** | *gain* |
| t7 | implication | 2 | *finding* |
| t14 | substantial | 2 | *costs* |
| t16 | negligible | 2 | *difference* |
| t21 | reluctant | 2 | *sign* |
| t6 | assumption | 1 + the whole frame *on the … that … would* | *start, would* |
| t23 | persistent | 1 | *years* |

The comparison is what makes the number mean something: `academic-verbs`'
worst item scores 3 and the shared word is incidental (*access*, *budget*). Here
the shared word **is the cue the item turns on**, and the explanations say so —
t8's names *"among excavators"*, t15's names the comparison, t4's names *below*.

This is the defect that killed two previous topics, arriving in a milder form:
not a shared scenario, but a shared collocation printed two or three times in
the lesson the `check` is served inside.

### D · Does the lesson teach the word, or teach around it?

All thirty members get a stated meaning — this passes, and `Certainty &
Doubt`'s two-sense treatment of `apparent` is the best piece of lexical
teaching in either topic even though nothing tests it. Two exceptions:

- **`considerable`** is defined by its company (*"substantial ile neredeyse eş
  anlamlıdır"* plus a tendency toward abstract degrees), and is never keyed.
- **`magnitude`** is defined with a false restriction (§1).

### E · Dependent prepositions and word formation

**§3.3's hole is not here.** The `forms` blocks teach `constraint on`,
`reluctant to`, `implication for`, `consensus on` — all four of the cases
`exam-vocabulary.md` names — plus `exemption from`, `criterion for`,
`obligation to + V`, `on the assumption that`, `evidence for/of`, `indication
of/that`, `indifferent to`, `cautious about`, `crucial to/for`, `beyond the
scope of`, `above/below the threshold`, `by a … margin`.

The hole is one step further on: **only t18 is decided by any of them.** Every
other blank is decided by meaning, with the governing preposition either absent
or already fixed by the frame. So the corpus teaches the patterns and measures
none of them, and two of them (`reluctant to`, the `an evidence` countability
rule) are taught with a whole `pitfall` and a whole `decision` rule that fire on
nothing.

No `forms` block anywhere shows a derivational family — §3.3 ranks word
formation the largest gap of all. Record it; it is a new-topic decision, not
something to bolt onto these six.

### F · One role, repeated

The un-keyed fifth member is an option in **4/4** items in three of the six
categories — `extent`, `indication`, `apparent` — and 3/4 in the rest. Within a
category worked as a set, the word that is always on the paper is never the
answer. This falls out of kickoff rule 4 and is the supervisor's call rather
than any lesson's defect, but it is worth deciding before the rule is applied
to the next twelve sets.

  fire on the right item, and t13 (a tiny quantity that is `crucial`) is the

  uniquely, load-bearing at t18. The `provision` imbalance is a coverage

  the giveaways.
