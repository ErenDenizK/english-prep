# Lesson review — `academic-verbs`

Sufficiency pass, six lessons against their own 24 questions. **Not blind:**
this pass reads the keys, because the question it asks — *does the lesson
contain what its category's items require* — cannot be asked without them.
The blind item pass is running separately; nothing here is an agreement rate
and nothing here is a `D1`/`D6` judgement about an item in isolation.

**Method.** Each lesson's `decision` block was run as a literal checklist over
its own category's four items: which rule fires, on which word, and whether
anything else in the lesson eliminates the key. Every English string in a
lesson was then compared against every paragraph in its category.

**Verdicts.**

| Category | Verdict | Blocking defect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change & Emergence | **does not ship** | The lesson eliminates t1's key three times over, and its `decision` R4 fires on a distractor |
| Cause & Consequence | ships | — |
| Claim & Concede | ships | — |
| Examine & Establish | ships | — |
| Sustain & Restrict | ships | — |
| Allocate & Withhold | ships | — |

Five of six reach every key. One does not, and it does not by a wide margin.

## 1 · Change & Emergence (emerge / evolve / shift / decline / fade) — does not ship

### Blocking · L1 + D8 · the lesson forbids the key of `academic-verbs-t1`

t1 keys `emerged`: a bridge parapet stood under a reservoir *"for half a
century"*, the water dropped, and *"the parapet ____ above the surface
**again**"*.

The lesson says three times that this is not `emerge`:

- `contrast` b1: *"emerge :: Daha önce görünmeyen ya da bilinmeyen bir şey
  **ilk kez** ortaya çıkar. **Öncesinde ortada o şey yoktur.**"*
- `pitfall` b8 `why`: *"**Öncesinde o şey yoksa emerge kullanılır.**"*
- `decision` R1: *"Cümle, **öncesinde ortada olmayan** bir şeyin **ilk kez**
  göründüğünü söylüyorsa … => emerge"*

The parapet existed throughout and the paragraph says so twice (*"covered the
old stone bridge"*, *"again"*). R1 does not fire. A learner who has read b1
strikes `emerged` out.

Worse, a rule fires on a distractor. `decision` R4 — *"Cümle ölçülebilir bir
miktar, oran ya da eski bir düzeyle karşılaştırma veriyorsa ve yön aşağıysa =>
decline"* — matches *"the water dropped so far"* exactly, and `declined` is an
option. The item's own explanation concedes the point: *"'Declined' … su
seviyesi için doğru olurdu ama boşluğun öznesi parapet."* The lesson never
tells the learner to check the subject of the blank; nothing in the six blocks
does that work.

The item's `tip` quietly repairs the lesson — *"'Emerge' bir şeyin ilk kez **ya
da yeniden** görünür hale gelmesidir"* — and that repair appears nowhere in
the lesson. A learner meeting t1 as a `check` inside this lesson meets a `tip`
that contradicts the `contrast` block three screens above.

### Blocking · untrue claim about English

*"Öncesinde ortada o şey yoktur"* is false about `emerge`. Its core sense is
to come into view from a concealed or enclosed position, and the thing exists
the whole time: *the sun emerged from behind the cloud*, *she emerged from the
building*, *the submarine emerged*. First appearance is one use, not the
definition.

**Correction:** *emerge* = become visible or known — either for the first time
or from behind something that was hiding it. What it excludes is the gradual
internal change of an already-visible thing, which is `evolve`; that is the
contrast the lesson actually wants, and it survives the correction intact.

### Worth fixing · untrue claim · `decline` is narrowed to counted quantities

*"decline :: Ölçülebilir bir miktar, oran ya da düzey düşer. **Cümle bir sayı
ya da eski bir düzeyle karşılaştırma taşır.**"*, inherited by R4.

`decline` applies freely to quality and condition with no number anywhere:
*his health declined*, *standards declined*, *the neighbourhood declined*, *her
influence declined*. A learner who has learned R4 will not fire it on *"her
health declined steadily through the winter"* — a plausible exam sentence.

**Correction:** the direction is down and the subject is a quantity, level or
condition; a number in the sentence is a strong cue, not a requirement. The
real boundary against `fade` is whether the thing has intensity/visibility
rather than a level, which the lesson already states well.

### Worth fixing · L5 ×2 · untested caveats

- `pitfall` b9 teaches that `decline` is intransitive in this sense (*"The
  reforms declined the number of accidents"* → *reduced*). `declined` is a
  distractor in t1, t3 and t4 and is never offered in a transitive frame, so
  the trap never springs. It is also the one place a Turkish speaker's
  *azaldı/azalttı* really bites, which is what makes leaving it untested a
  waste rather than a saving.
- `examples` b5 teaches `decline`'s second sense (*"She declined the offer"*).
  No item touches it.

### Note · the set is untested at its own centre

`evolve` is the un-keyed fifth member (a distractor in t1, t2, t3), which is
kickoff rule 4 working as designed. But `evolve` is also what b1, b6, b8 and R3
spend most of their words on, and the only item where the evolve/shift choice
is live (t2) is settled by one word, *"abrupt"*.

### Note · giveaways, low

`pitfall` b7 `right` — *"The number of applicants declined last year."* —
against t3 (key `declined`, *"…year after year"*, *"the last two classrooms"*):
shares the key and *last/year*. `examples` b5 *"Attendance declined for three
years."* likewise. Both are structural echoes, not shared scenarios. Nothing
here approaches the giveaway that killed earlier topics.

## 2 · Cause & Consequence (trigger / prompt / undermine / reinforce / accelerate) — ships

All four keys are reached. R2→t5, R4→t7, R5→t8 fire cleanly and nothing fires
on a distractor.

### Worth fixing · L1 · R3's second clause does not hold at `academic-verbs-t6`

R3: *"Tepki bir kişinin ya da kurumun aldığı bilinçli bir karar veya işlemse
ve **cümlede o özne varsa** => prompt"*.

t6: *"It was a local reporter's photograph … that finally ____ an independent
survey of the whole block."* The party that decided to commission the survey
is not in the sentence — only the survey is. The first half of R3 fires, the
second half fails, and the learner reaches `prompted` by eliminating the other
three rather than by the rule. Either drop the clause or write it as *"tepkinin
kendisi düşünülmüş bir işlemse (bir soruşturma, bir inceleme)"*, which is what
the item actually gives.

### Worth fixing · L5 · the category's headline contrast is never a live choice

`text` b6, `pitfall` b8 and two of the five decision rules are built on
trigger vs prompt. The two verbs are never in the same option set as a live
choice: t5 keys `triggered` with no `prompt` among the options, t6 keys
`prompted` with no `trigger`, and they co-occur only in t7, where the key is
`undermine`.

I think this was deliberate — b6 admits that for an official inquiry *"iki
fiil de savunulabilir"*, so pairing them would have produced a second
defensible answer. The avoidance is right; the consequence is that the
distinction the lesson teaches hardest is not measured anywhere, and should be
recorded as such rather than assumed covered.

### Worth fixing · L5 ×2 · two more untested caveats

- `pitfall` b7 (`accelerate` + a counted quantity → *increase*). `accelerated`
  is a distractor three times and is eliminated every time by *"there is no
  process under way"*, never by the quantity trap.
- `pitfall` b9 (`reinforce` takes an NP, not a bare that-clause). No item's
  blank is followed by a that-clause. t8's *"____ what the residents had
  believed"* is a free relative NP and does not spring it.

### Note · over-strong claim about English

`pitfall` b8 `why`: *"trigger nesne olarak kişi ya da kurum + to almaz"*.

`trigger + NP + to-infinitive` is standard in scientific and medical register:
*the allergen triggers the immune system to release histamine*. What is odd is
specifically an institution deliberating — *trigger the ministry to review* —
and that is because `trigger` excludes deliberation, not because the frame does
not exist. Restrict the claim to the reason that is actually true.

### Note · draft checker

`npm run draft` warns that t6 and t8 offer an identical option set
(`prompted / undermined / reinforced / accelerated`). Two of the four items
therefore draw from one pool.

## 3 · Claim & Concede (assert / imply / concede / dispute / acknowledge) — ships

The cleanest procedure/corpus fit in the topic alongside §4: R1→t10, R2→t9,
R3→t12, R4→t11, one rule per item, no rule firing on a distractor, and each
item's deciding span (*"repeats the claim twice more"*, *"never says"*,
*"Under questioning … finally"*, *"published their objections"*) is named by
exactly one rule.

### Worth fixing · untrue claim · `pitfall` b8's `wrong` is correct English

```
WRONG: The committee disputed the proposal for two hours.
RIGHT: The committee discussed the proposal for two hours.
```

*"The committee disputed the proposal for two hours"* is well-formed and
idiomatic; it means the committee contested it. The pitfall is trying to teach
*dispute ≠ tartışmak (discuss)*, which is a real Turkish interference — but it
does it by marking an acceptable English sentence as an error, which teaches
the learner to reject a sentence they should accept. `wrong` and `right` here
differ in the verb and therefore in the *meaning*, not in correctness, which is
not what a `pitfall` is for.

### Worth fixing · L5 ×2 · untested caveats, both built on non-members

- b9 (`imply` vs `infer`): `infer` appears in no option in the corpus.
- b8 (`dispute` vs `discuss`): `discuss` appears in no option in the corpus.

Both are good teaching. Neither is measured, and both spend a `pitfall` slot on
a word the category never offers.

### Note · internal inconsistency in `forms`

The `acknowledge` row's `use` claims a V-ing complement — *"Nesne: isim,
that-cümlesi ya da **V-ing**"* — while its `pattern` (`S + acknowledge + N /
that-clause`) and `example` show neither. Either show it or drop it from `use`;
the row is read as a formula.

## 4 · Examine & Establish (assess / determine / verify / estimate / monitor) — ships

The strongest lesson of the six. All five rules fire, one per item, none on a
distractor, and each item hands the rule its own trigger: *"what had started
it"* (R3), *"what the applicant has written"* + *"turns out not to match"*
(R1), *"impossible"* + *"about"* (R2), *"around the clock"* + *"twelve-month
record"* (R5). `text` b6's admitted assess/estimate overlap **is** tested, at
t15, and the item settles it with *"about"* — which is how an overlap should be
handled.

### Worth fixing · L5 · `pitfall` b9 is untested

b9 teaches monitor vs determine (*"Researchers monitored the cause of the
failure"*). The two never share an option set: t13 offers no `monitor`, t16
offers no `determine`.

### Note · role · `assess` is present in all four items and keyed in none

The un-keyed fifth member here appears in **4/4** option sets. Across a
category done in one sitting, the word that is always there is never the
answer. See the topic-level note; this is kickoff rule 4's shape, not this
lesson's defect.

## 5 · Sustain & Restrict (maintain / preserve / restrict / suspend / abandon) — ships

R5→t17, R3→t18, R1→t19, R2→t20. All four reached, nothing certified wrongly.

### Worth fixing · the taught pattern misparses this category's own item

`forms` b3 and `pitfall` b8 teach `restrict + N + to + N` as *the limit is what
follows `to`*: *"Access is restricted to staff"* = *"erişim yalnızca personele
açıktır"*.

t18 is *"the warden simply ____ access **to the eastern half of the beach**,
and walkers are asked to keep to the marked line."* Under the pattern the
learner has just been taught, that reads as *"the eastern half is the only part
you may enter"* — the opposite of what the paragraph means (the terns nest
there; walkers are kept off it). Here the `to`-phrase is locative, not the
limit.

The key is unaffected — `restricts` wins on either parse — so this is not
blocking. But t18 is served as a `check` **inside this lesson**, three blocks
after b8, and it is the one place the learner will try the pattern out.

### Worth fixing · L5 · `pitfall` b8's preposition is never tested

`restrict … to` vs `for` decides nothing anywhere: every blank in this category
is the bare verb, and no option differs from another by a preposition.

### Note · L1 · the rules are stated over the wrong noun for t17

R4 and R5 are written about *"korunan şey"* — the thing being protected. t17
turns on the **object of the blank**: the seed vault does `preserve` seeds, and
it does so by `maintain`ing a temperature, so the deciding question is whether
the blank's object is a level or a value. The item's explanation makes exactly
this move; the `decision` block never tells the learner to look at the object.

## 6 · Allocate & Withhold (allocate / distribute / assign / withhold / retain) — ships

R2→t21, R1→t22, R4→t23, R5→t24. All four reached.

`docs/research/exam-vocabulary.md` §3.2 pointed the reviewer here first. The
measurement below supports the concern without reaching a blocking verdict.

### Worth fixing · polarity does most of the work, and at t24 it does all of it

Every option set splits into giving verbs (`allocate`/`distribute`/`assign`)
and holding verbs (`withhold`/`retain`), and the paragraph's polarity is
readable before any lexical discrimination:

| item | giving options | holding options | after polarity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| t21 | allocates, distributes | withholds, retains | 2 live |
| t22 | assigned, allocated | withheld, retained | 2 live |
| t23 | assigned, distributed | withheld, retained | 2 live |
| **t24** | allocates, assigns, distributes | **retains** | **1 live** |

t24 is settled by *"an author who wants to reprint her own chapter … has to
write and ask permission"* — the university kept it — and `retains` is the only
holding-back option on the paper. No discrimination among the set is required
or measured.

### Worth fixing · L1 · t24's explanation teaches a sense the lesson denies

t24's explanation and its `assigns` note turn on the legal sense: *"'Assigns'
hukuk dilinde tam tersini söyler — bir hakkı başkasına devretmek."*

The lesson has `assign` as *"Bir görev, rol ya da yer belirli bir kişiye veya
ekibe verilir. **Nesne bir miktar değil, bir iştir.**"*, two `forms` rows for
tasks, and R1 to match. A learner who studied eliminates `assigns` correctly
(a copyright is not a task) and is then told by the explanation that `assign`
means transferring a right — a sense that is nowhere in the lesson, on a word
that is one of its five members.

### Worth fixing · L5 · `pitfall` b9 is untested

`withhold … from`, not `to`. No blank in this category is followed by the
recipient's preposition.

### Note

`distribute` is the un-keyed member (distractor in t21, t23, t24) and it is the
member the lesson can least easily select with a general-interest paragraph —
which is §3.2's point, arriving from the other direction.

## Topic-level findings

### Giveaways — measured, and low

Every English string in each lesson against every paragraph in its category,
scoring a hit when a lesson string shares the item's **key** *and* a content
word with the paragraph:

| item | key | lesson string | shared |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| t18 | restricts | b2 contrast ex. / b8 `wrong` / b8 `right` | *access* (×3) |
| t21 | allocates | b5 examples / b8 `right` | *budget* (×2) |
| t3 | declined | b7 `right` / b5 examples | *last, year* (×2) |
| t5 | triggered | b3 forms ex. *"The news triggered a sell-off."* | *sell* |
| t6 | prompted | b3 forms ex. *"The letter prompted officials to act."* | *letter* |
| t19 | suspended | b3 forms ex. *"The board suspended the trial."* | *trial* |
| t23 | withheld | b3 forms ex. *"They withheld payment from the supplier."* | *payment* |
| t16 | monitor | b3 forms ex. *"Staff monitor the system daily."* | *system* |

The worst is three lesson strings sharing one incidental noun. No lesson string
shares a scenario with a paragraph, and the `examples` blocks are **freshly
written** — 0 of 32 example sentences is a copy of a `contrast` example. Compare
`academic-nouns-adjectives`, where that number is 31 of 32 and the shared words
are the deciding collocates. This topic passes the check that killed two earlier
topics.

### `optionNotes` — pass

72 wrong options, 72 notes, none over 160 characters, none on a key. Every note
glosses its own word first and then says what the paragraph does instead:
*"Zaten süren bir süreci hızlandırmak. Sızıntıdan önce ortada bir satış dalgası
yok."* That is the field doing its job. No note in this topic merely
re-describes the key.

### Does the lesson teach the word, or teach around it?

All thirty members get a stated meaning. One exception, and it is the topic's
blocking defect: **`emerge` is defined by a precondition** (*"öncesinde ortada
o şey yoktur"*) rather than by what it means, and t1 keys the use the
precondition excludes. `fade` is likewise defined partly by what it cannot take
(no numbers) — harmless, because its positive gloss is also given.

### Dependent prepositions and word formation

**Not a hole in this topic.** The `forms` blocks carry `emerge from`,
`evolve from/into`, `shift from … to`, `decline by`, `fade away`, `prompt sb to
V`, `accelerate` (both frames), `assert/imply/concede that`, `assess as`,
`determine wh-`, `estimate at`, `restrict to`, `allocate to/for`, `distribute
to/among`, `assign to` + the ditransitive, and `withhold from`. Two of them get
their own `pitfall` (`restrict to`, `withhold from`).

The gap is one step further on: **no item is decided by any of them.** Every
blank is the bare verb with the governing preposition either absent or on the
far side of the blank, so nothing in the corpus measures whether the patterns
were learned. Two `pitfall` slots (b8 in §5, b9 in §6) are spent on prepositions
that never appear in an option.

No lesson shows a derivational family — `exam-vocabulary.md` §3.3 ranks word
formation the largest gap. Record it; it is a topic-level decision, not
something to bolt onto these six.

### One role, repeated across the topic

In each category the un-keyed fifth member is an option in three or four of the
four items: `evolve` 3/4, `accelerate` 3/4, `acknowledge` 3/4, **`assess`
4/4**, `preserve` 3/4, `distribute` 3/4. A learner working a category as a set
can learn that the word which is always on the paper is never the answer. This
falls straight out of kickoff rule 4 and is the supervisor's call, not any one
lesson's defect — but it is worth knowing before the same rule is applied to
the next twelve sets.

## Lessons I could not break

  directions and found no misfire, no gap and no untaught requirement beyond
  the untested b9 pitfall. The overlap the lesson admits is the overlap the

  pitfall's `wrong`, not the teaching.

  what is *not* measured, not about what the lesson gets wrong.
